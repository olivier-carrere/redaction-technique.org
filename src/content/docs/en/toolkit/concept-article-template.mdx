:::tip[Template]
This is a **starting point to copy and adapt**, not a worked example. Delete any section that doesn't apply — see [Standardized page layouts](/en/toolkit/) for when to skip a section entirely.
:::

Use this structure for an article that explains **what something is** and **why it matters** — not how to do something (use the [task template](../task-article-template/)) and not a lookup table of options (use the [reference template](../reference-article-template/)).

```markdown
---
title: "<Concept name>"
description: "<One sentence: what the reader will understand after reading this>"
---

## Definition

<One or two sentences defining the concept in plain language. A reader
skimming only this paragraph should walk away with a correct, if
incomplete, understanding.>

## Why it matters

<The consequence of understanding — or not understanding — this concept.
What breaks, costs more, or gets confusing without it?>

## Key principles

<The two to four ideas that make up the concept. A bulleted list or a
short table both work well here.>

## Examples

<At least one concrete example. Abstract concepts without an example
are the single most common cause of reader confusion.>

## Trade-offs

<Optional. Include only if the concept involves a real choice — when
would someone deliberately choose NOT to apply this concept?>

## Related concepts

- [<Related article 1>](<link>)
- [<Related article 2>](<link>)
```

## When to skip a section

- No genuine trade-off exists → drop **Trade-offs** entirely rather than inventing a weak one.
- The concept is simple enough that "why it matters" is obvious from the definition → merge the two.
- Never skip **Definition** or **Examples** — they're what make a concept article usable rather than just impressive-sounding.
