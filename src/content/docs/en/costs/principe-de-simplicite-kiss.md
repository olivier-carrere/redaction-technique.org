---
title: "KISS Principle of Simplicity"
description: "The KISS principle (Keep it simple stupid) is a general engineering principle that advocates simplicity to improve reliability, maintenance and scalability."
---

The <abbr title="Keep it simple stupid">KISS</abbr> principle is not specific to **technical writing**. It's a general engineering principle, which postulates that any hardware or virtual object built by man is more reliable and easier to maintain and evolve if its complexity is deliberately reduced. This is the principle emphasized antiphrastically by the Shadocks: *Why keep it simple when you can keep it complicated?

It was this principle that prevailed in the design of *Swatch* watches, whose specifications stipulated that they had to carry half as many parts as their counterparts. The result: watches that are less expensive, more reliable and available in ever-changing versions.

What has worked spectacularly well for watches can be applied with equal success to **technical documentation**. Whatever the format used, the **technical writer** can just as easily construct an elegantly structured document, as a gas factory. With **DITA XML**, all you have to do is avoid centralizing *conref* or over-nesting them. With **FrameMaker**, it's even simpler, with few safeguards in place: the multiplication of styles and *overrides* can quickly render any document unmanageable.

Similarly, when formulating sentences, the **technical editor** must always keep the KISS principle in mind. It's easy to construct convoluted sentences that are more indicative of a lack of understanding of the subject than of refined writing. Constructing a simple sentence requires an effort to understand the subject. It's then easy to write the content and make it comprehensible to the recipient. This is a fundamental aspect of the technical writer's job. The **technical writer** adds real value to the product he or she is documenting.
